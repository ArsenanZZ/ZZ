Louisville, Kentucky — Four years after my first U.S. marathon, I returned to Floyds Fork for a hometown loop that closed the PhD chapter.

Foreword｜Home final chapter · Doctor’s final game

The first official marathon I ran in the United States was the Louisville Marathon.

At the end of 2020, the epidemic was getting serious. I have been in the United States for almost a year. I ran my first U.S. marathon wearing a mask in the slightly scorching autumn weather in the city. I ran very slowly and was very exposed to the sun.

Four years later, in the fall of 2024, I signed up for this competition again. But this time: I have more hair, my steps are lighter, my shoes are better, my identity is different, my doctoral thesis defense has been completed, and I am just waiting for the graduation ceremony. Awesome, Dr. ZZ’s academic marathon is coming to an end.

This game is not part of Dr. ZZ’s Run50 “New State Check-in”. But for Dr. ZZ, it was the curtain call of a five-year doctoral career.

The Louisville Marathon's track is located at Floyds Fork Park on the city's east side. There is no hustle and bustle of the big city, just winding asphalt paths, woods and small bridges. It’s perfect for “thinking about life”, and I finally crossed the finish line on a lawn, Egg Lawn. It is a USATF certified Boston Qualifier track.

However, to be honest, BQ has little to do with me, at least for the time being. But this horse has witnessed two nodes in my life, like a complete loop: 2020: the starting point of American horse racing; 2024: the end of my doctoral career.

It can be said that it has a beginning and an end. This Louisville Marathon has witnessed me go from masks to tassels, from a newbie in American horse racing, to now I have run in 22 states. The speed is still not fast, but I am still a bit of a "legendary" "Doctor" runner.

Chapter 1｜Start early in the morning·The freshness of ten miles

The autumn in Lucheng has turned yellow, the leaves are falling one by one, and the ground creaks when stepped on. The sunrise on the horizon slowly lights up, stacked with the yellow fields, and it looks quite comfortable.

I parked my car in the grass parking lot and followed the crowd towards the starting point. The competition is not big, but there are a lot of people, and the red arch can be seen at a glance. Everyone stood there in twos and threes, warming up and chatting. I wore an old jacket and planned to throw it away when the heat got too hot.

After starting, the air was cool and fresh. It was not crowded during the run and everyone had their own rhythm. In the distance, the sky is high and the grass is wide, and I am moving forward in an indomitable way, just like my state of mind when I first entered the Ph.D.

The sun had just emerged, and the river was shining with golden light. Two miles into the woods and still see some green. At three miles, the track passes under a high iron bridge, which is the Norfolk Southern Railway Bridge. There are photos and flowers of a girl under the bridge, which should be a memorial to the community.

After about five miles, the sun rose completely, and there were large reed swamps on both sides. The sun shone from the side, and the shadow was stretched very long on the ground. It felt like I was running with myself.

In a blink of an eye, we reached 10 miles. I was not running very slowly during this period. After 10 miles, it became a bit dazzling and the sun was shining directly on me.

Chapter 2｜Hitting a wall · Feeling energetic again

The run was going pretty well before half way. The watch jumped to 13 miles, just under two hours. For me, who hasn’t been training well recently and whose belly is still growing, this result is pretty good.

But halfway through, the scenery began to repeat itself: the same woods, the same riverbanks, the same meadows. The audience was sparse and there were fewer and fewer runners. All that's left is me and my own breathing. The sun is rising higher and higher, making people feel stuffy with the heat. Mile 16, short of breath, legs like lead.

This feels like halfway through a PhD: funding cuts, simulated crashes, and milk for kids. What's even worse is that the boss will throw in a project at any time and ask you to put down all the work you are doing and work overtime to write a notebook. It's not nutritious. I want to complain, but I can only do it in silence. I have to pretend to eat the boss's fragrant flatbread, which is endless aftertaste.

When I was confused, a familiar face suddenly appeared on the track, Gleen, still looking the same, holding a 5-hour Pacer sign and happily leading the team forward. I said hello to him, and my mood suddenly returned to normal. Just like meeting a colleague during the Ph.D. program and saying, "You haven't graduated yet," I instantly felt balanced.

Moving forward, the supplies finally paid off. At mile 20, my body seemed to suddenly switch modes, my rhythm stabilized, and my mind became clear.

Chapter 3｜Crossing the finishing line · Little red flower and black gold badge

After 20 miles, the track suddenly became lively. On both sides of a supply point, red berries are growing in full bloom, like little red flowers given to every runner. I stopped to take a sip of water and asked a volunteer to take some "black and red" photos for me.

The background is red, which perfectly matches my black Adidas Runner (AR) clothes. That piece of clothing has a story. It was a prize I received during an AR running event in Shanghai in 2019. It was also the equipment I wore when I ran the Louisville Marathon for the first time. It has been five years old and the quality is still excellent.

There were sporadic spectators on the roadside, holding signs to cheer us on. Some shouted "Pain is temporary, Strava is forever". The sun was a bit hot, but I was "physically and mentally healthy" and my steps were not messy.

Further on, the mile 26 sign finally came into view. The final arch is not grand and there are not many people. But after running that moment, I still applauded myself. The medal hangs heavily around my neck, with gold letters on a black background and flashing 26.2 and Louisville Marathon. This is the medal for the final battle of my PhD.

Five years, starts from here and ends from here. My first marathon in the United States was the Louisville Marathon, and the last marathon of my PhD career was also the Louisville Marathon. A complete circle, with a beginning and an end.

The five years I spent studying for a Ph.D. were absolutely amazing: I ran 30 marathons in 22 states in the United States; I published 12 papers; I wrote a project for my boss and won $918,734; I developed all-round morally, intellectually, physically, artistically, and physically.

I am worthy of my boss. In terms of scientific research, I have not missed a beat; in terms of life, I have also fulfilled my professional ethics.

I am also grateful to my boss, who finally let me become Dr. ZZ. He was not at a loss as he also got tenure and could change jobs.

The pressure you should have should be left in the past; the gratitude you should have should be kept in your heart. At the end of these five years, I have not lost anything, and neither has he. That's it, pretty good.

Chapter 4｜Final Chapter of the Doctor·Testimony of Family

In early December, my parents came to the United States together with Dad and Mom Wang.

This is the “big plan” that Siqi and I have been planning for a long time, and it can also be regarded as the last addition to the five-year PhD life.

My parents landed in Chicago. At that moment, my parents and I had not seen each other for five years. When I saw them for the first time, I felt that nothing had changed. It's good, no need to sensationalize.

Dad and Mom Wang helped take care of them all the way from China to the United States. It was really great! We went all the way to an Airbnb to rest for a few days, and also went to Chinatown for Chinese food. It was bustling and bustling, as if the five years of distance had been erased all of a sudden.

Then the family drove five hours back to Louisville. The car was crowded, but even more lively. When I got home, my life suddenly had a "taste of home": my mother was the first to cook, cooking for us in different ways every day, which was more practical than any scientific research results.

In a few days, it was time for the graduation ceremony.

First, there was a special performance for doctoral students. In the school auditorium, the atmosphere was solemn and the music was solemn. My tutor happened to be absent, so Teacher Yang plucked my ears for me, and I shook hands with the principal and dean. In the audience, my parents were also there, as was Dr. Wang’s wife. There was also “professional photography” from Father Wang and blessings from Mother Wang.

I took my family around the campus, which has been my base for five years. From the overtime laboratory to the school’s fountain gate, I am familiar with it all.

In the evening, there was also a school-wide ceremony at the Yum Center in the city, where KFC was named. The scene was very American: grand, but a little chill. The graduates walked onto the podium, some singing, some dancing, with a bit of playful pride. My dad even sneaked up to the stage and took a photo of me.

I'm very proud of this whole day. My parents must be too.

This doctor's hat is both secure and fun to wear.

Chapter 5｜Christmas Month·Family playing in the United States

In December, the United States enters the Christmas season. I have also entered "accompanying mode" here. After working for a week, I quickly asked for leave and took my parents, Father and Mother Wang, for a walk.

We went to an outlet, my parents’ first time shopping in the United States, to look at brands and try on clothes.

Then we participated in the Ugly Sweater Run. Although we arrived late and missed the big group, it was still fun to walk in the winter park wearing fancy sweaters. It's just that the wind in Louisville was a bit strong, which gave Mrs. Wang a cold.

Also attended Jackie and Scott's wedding. There were only a dozen or so people in the park where I ran the Louisville Marathon. It was simple and straightforward, but also very warm.

Then a small trip: drive to see the largest waterfall in Kentucky: Cumberland Falls, known as the "Niagara of the South." It was a little rainy that day, the water vapor was filled, and the waterfall roared.

We stayed in a cabin in the mountains at night, and the Christmas tree was sparkling. Siqi also prepared uniform pajamas in advance. The family took photos in plaid suits, which instantly gave the feeling of an American family movie.

Of course, there are also episodes in the joy. I flew the drone on a whim and accidentally flew onto the roof. Fortunately, my dad and Siqi helped together, and the three of them cooperated and successfully fished it out. The team spirit of the doctor's family was clearly demonstrated.

We also visited the first KFC store, which was part restaurant and part museum.

Later, when I sent my parents to the Chicago airport, I took them to the U.S. Air Force Museum in Dayton to see airplanes and Apollo. I tried Indian food in the evening, which was acceptable to my parents.

A month passed quickly, and we parted ways at the Chicago airport. I waved my hand through the security glass, and I’ll see you back home next time!

Postscript · Little Red Flower

Five years of a PhD is like an ultra-marathon.

There are the fresh ten miles, the eighteen miles where we hit the wall, the twenty miles where we endured, and the black gold medal when we crossed the finish line.

I am very proud of this journey. Maybe this is the meaning of studying for a doctorate. It gave me strong resilience and self-confidence, and made me healthier physically and mentally. In the end, I successfully became Dr. ZZ.

This December graduation ceremony is even more special: my parents came to the United States for the first time, and Father and Mother Wang also came. During the Christmas month, we see waterfalls, stay in log cabins, go to the Air Force Museum, and run in ugly sweaters. The doctor's conclusion is not just a matter of one person, but a testimony of the whole family.

The medal is a small red flower, the academic cap is a small red flower, and the family reunion is also a small red flower.

These little red flowers reward me for running in the coldest winter and give me strength on days when I hit the wall.

The wind and snow in Colorado, the showers in the Himalayas.

The end of the world is full of cattle and sheep, the end point of the black and gold medal.

Give me a little red flower.
