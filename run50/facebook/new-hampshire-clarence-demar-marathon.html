Run50 State #22 | New Hampshire: Clarence DeMar Marathon

Keene, New Hampshire — From Niagara Falls into New England foliage, this was a small-town marathon through Keene, quiet roads, autumn forests, and a campus finish.

Foreword｜Autumn break is not a holiday · sneak away to see the waterfall

The end of September is called "autumn break" according to the school calendar. But things like autumn break only exist in notifications in our laboratory. Seeing that my graduation defense is approaching and I haven’t run a marathon in a month, I am wondering in my heart: Do I need to find a reason to go out and take a breath?

As usual, I opened RaceRaves and looked at the map, and found that there happened to be a highly rated marathon in New Hampshire: the Clarence DeMar Marathon. The only problem: it takes a full seventeen hours to drive there from Kentucky.

Together, 471 and I, since we have to run so far, we might as well "break it into parts". First go to see the world's most famous and largest waterfall in the United States: Niagara Falls, then go all the way north through the autumn colors of Vermont, and finally reach the small town of Keene, New Hampshire.

So, this "autumn break trip without autumn break" was decided.

The 22nd State · New Hampshire | The State of White Mountains and Maple Leaves

New Hampshire is located in the northeastern United States and is one of the six New England states. New England includes Maine, Vermont, New Hampshire, Massachusetts, Connecticut, and Rhode Island. This area is one of the earliest colonies in the United States, has a rich history, and has particularly distinctive four seasons.

New Hampshire itself is a small state, but it has outstanding features:

Mountain forests and lakes: The northern part of the state is home to the famous White Mountains. The highest peak, Mount Washington, is windy all year round and is famous for its changeable weather. There are many lakes in the central part of the state, such as Lake Winnipesaukee, which is a resort in the summer.

Fall Maple Leaves: New England is famous for its fall colors, and New Hampshire is a great place for foliage viewing. At the end of September and the beginning of October, the entire mountain forest turns into a picture of red, yellow and orange.

Small town atmosphere: There is no hustle and bustle of a big city. The largest city is Manchester and the capital is Concord. Our destination this time, Keene, is a university town with a quiet and cultural atmosphere.

In American running circles, the New England tradition is also very special. This is the birthplace of the Boston Marathon, and the oldest marathon culture in the United States emerged from here.

Chapter 1｜Niagara Falls·The shock in the mist

We set out from Louisville on Thursday night and stayed at the Red Roof Inn along the way. As Halloween is approaching, pumpkin decorations have been put up outside the hotel, and the atmosphere is quite lively. We continued driving early the next morning and finally arrived at Niagara Falls, NY, the city where Niagara Falls is located.

Next to the parking lot is the visitor center and shopping mall. There is an enthusiastic uncle holding a map and telling us how to walk and where to see the best scenery. Unfortunately, we arrived a little late and missed the boat ride. So I walked directly into Niagara Falls State Park.

This is the oldest state park in the United States. It was designed by Frederick Law Olmsted, the designer of Central Park. Trails and observation decks have been built in the park to facilitate visitors to get close to the waterfall.

We first went to the observation deck in front of the Tesla statue. From this angle, you can see the entire American Falls. The reason why there is a statue of Tesla is because he and Edison started the "War of Electricity" here. In the end, Tesla's alternating current solution won, and the first hydroelectric power station was built at Niagara Falls, which became a symbol of the electrical age.

Then we went to experience the "Cave of the Winds". Before entering, you must watch a video in the exhibition hall to introduce the geology of Niagara, the formation of the waterfall, and the story of hydropower generation.

Then get a yellow raincoat and take the elevator down to the wooden boardwalk at the foot of the waterfall. At that moment, it was like walking into a violent storm, with water splashing directly on my face, I couldn't open my eyes, and my shoes were soaked in a short time. Standing on the plank road, there was a roar in my ears, and the waterfall roared right in front of me. It was really shocking.

Going forward, you can also see Horseshoe Falls on the other side. Compared with the American Falls, it is wider and more spectacular. It is opposite Canada. It is said that the viewing angle is better from the Canadian side.

In the evening we found a Chinese restaurant nearby to eat and warm up a little.

Went back to the park to watch the night view in the evening. The waterfall looks dreamy under the colorful lights, and the water mist reflects the colors of blue, purple, and red, constantly changing. Standing at that moment, I felt that the shock of the day had been replaced by the dream of the night.

After seeing the night view, we continued driving and stayed at an Airbnb in Syracuse for the night to prepare for our journey north the next day.

Chapter 2 | Traveling through the autumn colors of Vermont · Pre-game party

After leaving Niagara, we stayed one night in Syracuse. The next morning it was sunny and the clouds were beautiful.

I don’t have much impression of Syracuse. I only know that it is the home of Syracuse University and that Anthony played here. My junior high school classmate Xiao Gao also studied here, so I feel a bit familiar.

Leaving Syracuse, the car slowly drove into Vermont. The end of September is the foliage season in New England, and the leaves all over the mountains and fields begin to turn red and yellow, with layers of colors. We found a place to get off the bus and took some photos.

After traveling all the way through Vermont, we finally arrived at our destination: the remote state of New Hampshire. In the afternoon, we first went to the university to pick up equipment. The location was Keene State College, a small local university.

There were not many people in the hall, everyone lined up quietly, and the atmosphere was quite relaxed.

After picking up our gear, we drove into the mountains and found an Airbnb cabin. The landlord is an old woman who lives alone. She keeps the house neat and tidy, and the autumn forest can be seen outside the window.

In the evening we went to a pre-marathon party. The buffet was homely, with spaghetti and meatballs. What was even more special was that there was a legendary guest speaker at the party, Kathrine Switzer, the first woman to officially register and finish the Boston Marathon.

She shared how she persevered and faced doubts back then, and encouraged us to treat marathon as a journey of life.

In the evening, when we returned to the cabin, it was completely dark. Only the lights of the wooden house were shining in the forest, and it was warm inside.

The old lady talked to us about her trip to China twenty years ago. And I didn’t know how to accurately introduce China now. At that moment, I suddenly realized that I hadn’t been back for a long time.

Chapter 3｜The starting line in the mountains and forests

The Clarence DeMar Marathon is the most representative marathon in New Hampshire. It starts from the community school in the small town of Gilsum and ends at Appian Way at Keene State College, where Clarence DeMar led his students to train.

Founded in 1978, the marathon is held every year in late September, just in time for the fall colors in New England. The route passes through mountains and forests, country roads, lakes and small towns.

Although it is downhill overall (the altitude drops from 800 feet to 500 feet), there are many ups and downs in the middle, such as a long slope at mile 15, and short and steep slopes near the cemetery at miles 22 and 24, so the overall difficulty is not low.

On the morning of the race, Siqi and I left a few words in my landlady’s notebook before setting off. Siqi first sent me to the starting point of the full marathon in Gilsum, and then went to Surry Mountain Beach, the starting point of the half marathon.

The starting point is not big, just at the intersection of the town, but everyone gathers together and the atmosphere is very cordial. I noticed a white-haired grandma wearing a white ribbon on her last leg of the 50-state marathon.

After starting the race, the temperature was a bit cool, so I took off the clothes I wore and quickly warmed them up, leaving them at the mile sign. The clothes were bought on Pinduoduo and they were very cheap.

There are woods on both sides of the track and the air is very fresh. At 3 miles, the woods became denser, and fallen leaves were spread out on the ground, making a rustling sound when stepped on. Several friends I met at the party last night were also on the track and said hello to each other.

At 8 miles, we reached Surry Mountain Lake. The lake is open, the mountains in the distance are already red and yellow, and the scenery is beautiful. This section of the road is relatively gentle and is the most relaxing section of the whole journey.

By mile 10, we were still in the countryside, the road was not wide, with woods and grassland on both sides. There were more spectators here than before, and local residents stood on the roadside to cheer. The supply station is also very prompt, with water and drinks available.

By mile 11, the course hit Surry Mountain Dam. The dam blocked the lake and the road surface was smooth, just like running on the lake. The lake is on the left, the woods are on the right, and the mountains of Monadnock can be seen in the distance. It was a bit cool due to the wind, but the scenery was very open and it was considered the most unique section of the whole journey.

Chapter 4｜Running and running in the autumn colors

The footsteps are still moving forward in the autumn colors of New Hampshire. From time to time, a few spectators appeared on the roadside, holding signs and ringing bells to cheer us up. When we turned the corner, we encountered cemeteries and stone monuments, and the atmosphere was quiet.

Fourteen miles ago, a "Superman" suddenly appeared beside the track, wearing a red cape and raising his fist, which was particularly conspicuous. I couldn't help but go up and take a picture. It felt like I was instantly buffed, affected by some kind of dark matter, and felt much lighter all of a sudden.

Continuing to run forward, I also met an aunt wearing Boston Marathon clothes. On the American circuit, Boston's logo is like a medal. Seeing her, I secretly thought: One day, I will rely on my strength to buy one.

At mile 17, I finally saw Siqi. She was in good condition and still capable. There were more spectators than before. There were children holding colored paper and residents shouting "good job" at the door. The atmosphere was lively.

Next is a path in the forest, with tall trees on both sides. Running along it feels like entering a natural tunnel. After holding on for a while longer, I reached mile 20 and my body started to feel a little heavy, but I was in good shape.

Chapter 5 | Stillness and movement in the cemetery

The track continues to wind forward, and the enthusiasm of the local residents remains the same. Sometimes we can meet a group of little girls who are training. They run past the intersection in groups. When they see us passing by, they will smile and applaud everyone. After passing through a residential area composed of several small white houses, I came to the most special section of the competition: a large cemetery.

This area actually consists of Woodland Cemetery and Greenlawn Cemetery, which is the oldest and largest cemetery in Keene and is where many local celebrities and veterans are buried. Here, mile 22 stands among the tombstones. Rows of stone tablets stand neatly on the grass, which is both spectacular and quiet. The track runs specifically through the cemetery, with supply points and volunteers on the side.

The atmosphere was quiet, but not scary. Instead, it had a sense of solemnity and power.

Running here, you can feel a kind of peace, which seems to remind everyone: to live, you must run.

I couldn't help but think that Chinese and American cultures are really different in this regard. In China, running into a cemetery may be considered unlucky, but in the United States, cemeteries are often part of the community, where people walk, run, and even have picnics. For them, this is a way to commemorate and connect with life. Running among the tombstones makes people feel that they are with history.

Here I met Siqi for the second time, we said hello and moved on. Finally running out of the cemetery, there was a cheering team with children dancing outside, which was in sharp contrast to the quiet atmosphere inside.

Then 25 miles ago I met Siqi for the third time. The routes of the full marathon and the half marathon were sometimes separated and sometimes overlapped, so we met three times in the same race, which was also a rare experience. There is only the last mile left in front of us, and the atmosphere of the finish line seems to be filling the air.

Chapter 6｜The last mile · Rushing to campus

The last mile is to run into Keene State College. There is a cheerleading team cheering at the school gate, and the sound is very lively.

Because this marathon is not a big one, I was the only one in the section before crossing the line. There were many spectators in the fences on both sides, so I felt like I had the whole audience’s cheers to myself.

I raised my hands and crossed the finish line. The volunteer aunt smiled and hung the medal around my neck. The medal is shaped like a frame around Strava's membership logo, with a silhouette of a runner crossing the finish line with arms raised against the backdrop of New Hampshire's mountains and lakes. The design is simple yet powerful.

Siqi and I met at the finish line and she also finished successfully. We stood on the grass and drank the hot soup provided by the organizing committee, and our bodies immediately felt a lot warmer. Then take a car to pick up the car at the starting point of her half marathon.

The half marathon starts at Surry Mountain Lake. This is a man-made lake on the Ashulote River, dammed by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers in the 1940s primarily for flood control.

The lake is large, with a beach and picnic area on one side. It is a summer vacation spot for locals. Behind it is an entire mountain forest reserve called Surry Mountain, with dense forests and undulating mountains. It is also a place where town residents like to hike and run.

We flew the drone. In the lens, the lake was as quiet as a mirror, reflecting all the clouds in the sky. The woods on the shore were dyed in red and yellow autumn colors.

It’s hard to come to New Hampshire, but seeing this scene, it feels like it’s worth the price.

Postscript · The end of the autumn journey

We left New Hampshire in the afternoon. I put on a blue long-sleeved commemorative shirt from this Clarence DeMar Marathon, feeling a little unsatisfied. Along the way, I was still thinking about the scenes I ran through in the morning: the quiet path in the mountain forest, the dam with the clouds reflecting on the lake, the solemn and peaceful atmosphere in the cemetery, and the enthusiastic cheering of the spectators on the roadside.

The return route was different. We went around New York and came to New Haven, where Yale University is located.

We stopped for a while in the city, found an Indian restaurant, and ordered Lamb Masala (mutton curry), naan and rice to replenish our energy. After dinner, I drove around the Yale campus. The lights outside the window reflected the red brick buildings and tall towers, giving me a sense of quiet beauty.

Founded in 1701, Yale University is one of the oldest Ivy League schools in the United States. There are many elegant Gothic buildings on the campus. There are many well-known alumni here, such as several U.S. presidents (Bush Jr., Clinton), secretaries of state (Hillary Clinton, John Kerry), and Cha Liangyong, also known as Jin Yong.

Driving through the campus, you can feel the calm and heavy aura, which seems to have a bit of martial arts flavor. Even if it is raining lightly outside, it does not affect its magnificence.

In the evening, we checked into a small hotel. The next morning, we continued driving back to Kentucky. Even though it happened to be autumn break, my boss still arranged a temporary meeting, leaving me nowhere to hide. The attendance control has been very tight recently.

But even so, I have no regrets about this trip to New Hampshire. In the 22nd state, running in the autumn colors of New England, the people and things I saw were very special.

After the race, I also found a running video of Penny Roll, the grandma who completed the 50-state challenge. The photo of me and "Superman" was taken from her video. I will still take more shots in the future. A small coincidence made me become a part of the legendary record.
